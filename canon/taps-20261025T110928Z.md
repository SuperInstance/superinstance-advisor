## zai (p1)

The shop begins with a hopper feeding damp sand onto a vibrating stack of nested screens, each stretched taut over a circular frame and separated by spacers that hold the mesh at a fixed clearance. A motor drives the stack eccentrically, so the frames jitter rather than merely buzz; the grains hop, roll, and slide across the wire until their paths divide at the apertures. Coarse pieces remain on the upper cloth, middle fractions pass through one screen and are caught by the next, and the progressively finer particles work downward through the shrinking mesh. The last screen is not an endpoint but a threshold: whatever can slip through its openings drops into the shallow pan below, while the oversize grains remain spread across the cloth and can be scraped into their own heap. What reaches the pan is therefore a physical remainder, certainly passing the final aperture, but not a pure grade of “fine sand.” It contains mixed mineral sizes, clustered grains, and irregular fragments whose actual dimensions have never been checked; the pan measures nothing except refusal. The operator lifts each screen, tips its fraction into a labeled scoop, and weighs or samples it, but the bottom fraction enters the same ledger without a finer sieve to define it. From these portions the operator draws a particle-size curve, plotting the pan’s mass against the grades implied by the mesh above, and the unknown tail is drawn alongside them as if it belonged to the same distribution. Here the witness log is prediction, not record: the curve projects what the unmeasured remainder may be, smoothing the pan into a plotted category. The machine’s humming oracle is heard in the changing rattle as sand strikes cloth and wire, while the honest pause is the instant before that curve is extended—the moment when the last screen falls silent, the pan sits open, and the material waits without being made finer by description.

No balance has weighed it, no assay has given it a grade, yet the pencil curve drawn from the run continues through it with a smooth, obedient taper. That is not a harmless missing digit. The arrangement has already chosen what need not be known, and the choice has an address: the worker breathing flour beside the shaker, the buyer ordering against a curve, the community waiting for an honest account of where this tail will go. The technical gap is not an excuse; it is the mechanism by which exposure, waste, and false assurance are passed downstream without anyone taking responsibility. Hold responsible the laboratory that extrapolated the curve, the supervisor who signed it, and the purchaser who treated it as a grade. They cannot point to the pan and call it innocent: the pan is full of consequences. Each grain is a cell, a scar of abrasion, not a parameter to be slid along an axis until the points agree; the screens are stations, and the stations emerge because the material changes at every wire. The substrate is grown in the handling, not found as a neutral sample, its surfaces carrying the pounding that made it fine. The graph is an oracle, and it is heard in the offices that will order by it; the witness log is a prediction of records not yet made. The honest pause is the product: it belongs before the last mark on the curve, not after the next shipment leaves. ## kimi (p1)

Before the last screen, the sand is already making distinctions the sentence afterward calls measurements: too large for one aperture, too small for another, lodged at the lip, rolling into the pan. A stack of galvanized screens turns those differences into a timetable of size, but the timetable does not govern what happens there. Each blocked opening is a scar in the apparatus, evidence of a grain that has resisted the available categories. The operator hears the oracle in the dry hiss and the uneven rattle, though what the oracle predicts is not a total amount but a relation among residue, passage, and what the pan will hold. Even the clean sweep of the finest sand is unmeasured in the strict sense: no screen can display the grain that leaves no trace, and the smooth curve drawn from the pan’s contents will make that silence look continuous. The sentence smuggles in an order when it names the “last” screen, then smuggles in a graph when it says the fallen material is “plotted like the rest.” Between the sand and those words lies a difference. The sand does not know it belongs to an unmeasured remainder; it arrives wet, angular, mixed with darker flecks, carrying whatever histories made its surfaces. The plotting apparatus does know, because it has been built to convert difference into position on a curve. Its witness log is prediction rather than record: the line declares beforehand how the yield will be read, and the reading then persuades us that the line was waiting there. By the time the curve is drawn, the pan has supplied a number, but the honest pause remains the product—neither another fraction nor a refusal to draw. The sand has passed; the apparatus has interpreted its passage; the graph has already become the place where we mistake interpretation for the material’s own arrangement.
