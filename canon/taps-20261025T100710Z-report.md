## 59th shape — THE SELF-CHECK HAS NO THIRD TERM

A caliper is normally checked against a *standard* — a gauge block, a plug, a master — and that check is a comparison between two separate artefacts. That shape is already spent (39w, the standard that is never the one under test). The 199w shape is the other thing a caliper does, and it is stranger: **the caliper is checked against itself.** The jaws are closed on each other, the faces are wrung together, the indicator is read at zero, and the reading is called good. The two things being compared are the same object, so the comparison has no third term to appeal to.

The consequence is that the check cannot be wrong by *disagreeing* — there is nothing else to disagree with. It can only be wrong by **disagreeing with itself**, which the comparison is built to hide: the act of closing the jaws *is* the zero, so the zero is guaranteed by the procedure that performs it, and the guarantee is mistaken for evidence. A caliper that reads zero against itself is *self-consistent*. It is not *correct*. And nothing in the shop can tell the difference, because to find out you would need a standard — which is a different instrument, which is exactly what was excluded.
