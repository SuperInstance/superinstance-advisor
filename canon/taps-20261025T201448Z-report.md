## 2. The 69th shape

> **THE CERTIFICATE IS THE MEAN OF THE ENDS, SO NO PIECE HOLDS THE VALUE IT IS GIVEN.**

Optical glass is not quarried and not forged. It is **melted**: a batch of raw
materials is fused in a furnace at about 1400 degrees, stirred, and cast. The
melt is a liquid, and a liquid has no shape, so the glass is a liquid that was
frozen while somebody decided what shape it should take.

The decisive property of optical glass is its **refractive index** — how much
light slows on entering the medium, which fixes how a surface made of it bends
a ray. And a second, the **Abbe number**, which is the **dispersion**: how
differently the glass bends different colours. A lens is a device for bending
light, so its behaviour is set by these two numbers and by the radii of its
surfaces.

Here is the part that is usually left out, and it is the whole shape.

**The refractive index of a delivery lot is NOT the refractive index of any
piece of glass in the lot.** The glassmaker measures the index of the **melt**:
samples are pulled from the melt as it is cast, one per casting, and each is
ground into a small prism and measured on a goniometer by the angle of minimum
deviation. The lot's certified index is then

```
n_lot = (n_max + n_min) / 2
```

— the **mean of the highest and lowest** index found in the lot. The number on
the test report, printed on every delivery note and quoted in every lens
designer's spreadsheet, is a value that **no piece of the glass has**. Every
piece in the lot is then checked against that midpoint, and each piece is
judged in specification when it agrees with a number that is not its own.

And the measurement that **would** reveal the mistake is the one never made on
the finished object. The lens is not tested for dispersion; it is tested for
**focal length**, which is fixed by the index **and** the radius — so a lens
made from the wrong glass can be brought to the right focal length by changing
the radius, and the focal length will not know. The lens passes. What the lens
actually is — its dispersion, its colour, its figure — is read from the sample
prism, which is a different piece, of a different size, with a different
thermal history, filed under a different number.

- **THE CERTIFICATE IS THE MEAN OF THE ENDS, SO NO PIECE HOLDS THE VALUE IT IS
  GIVEN.** The measurement is real, the comparison is real, and the conclusion
  is real — but the thing every piece is judged against is a **synthetic point
  that the batch has no member of**. The lot has a value and no piece of the
  lot has it.
- **THE FINISHED PIECE IS CERTIFIED BY A MEASUREMENT THE MISTAKE WOULD NOT
  AFFECT.** A wrong glass brought to the right focal length is a lens that
  passes every test it is given. The error does not hide because the test was
  not done; it hides because the test that **was** done cannot see it.
- **THE THING THAT WOULD REVEAL THE ERROR IS THE THING THAT IS NEVER MEASURED
  ON THE FINISHED OBJECT.** The index is measured on a sample pulled from the
  melt. The lens is certified by its focal length. The measurement that would
  tell you the lens is wrong is the one performed on a different piece of
  glass, on a different day, under a different number.

**The canon mirror.** A canon entry is certified by a number that no entry in
the canon has. The lot's mean is the canon's centre of gravity — a real
quantity, computed from real entries, that no individual entry possesses. The
reading is honest, the comparison is honest, and the certificate is honest,
and every entry is in specification against a centre the entries themselves do
not occupy. The honest pause is the product: the interval in which the
difference between the mean and the member is not measured is the interval in
which the reader is not lied to.
