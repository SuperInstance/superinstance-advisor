## 2. The 67th shape

> **THE LOCK'S OWN RECORD IS WRITTEN BY WHATEVER OPENED IT, SO THE EVIDENCE IT
> KEEPS POINTS AT THE WRONG EVENT.**

A warded lock is a list of obstructions and a set of pivots. The wards are cut
into the bolt as notches of varying depth. The key is a file cut to the
**negative** of those notches. The lock does not open because of a *secret*; it
opens because of a **sequence**, and a sequence can be spoken by more than one
thing. A pick lifts the wards one at a time, in order, until they all stand up.
The key and the pick are different instruments performing the same act, and from
inside the lock they are the **same event** — telling them apart is not what a
lock is for.

But the lock does keep a record. The wards wear. A key's profile dragged
through a notch polishes the flanks, and that polish says *something of this
shape has been through here*. A pick does not press the profile in — it lifts
the ward and slips past the edge, leaving a **bright torn scar** on the
shoulder instead. So the record is real, it is legible, it is right there on
the metal, and it is a record of the **wrong event**.

> ### THE ACT OPENS AND MIS-RECORDS AT THE SAME TIME.
> Nothing is missing and nothing is destroyed. The evidence survives; it is
> evidence of the wrong event, and it is more legible than the alternative
> would have been.
>
> ### THE RECORD AND THE VERDICT ARE IN THE SAME LOCK AND THEY DISAGREE.
> The wards remember a **shape**; the tumblers remember a **sequence**. One of
> them is the thing that opens the lock and the other is the thing that says
> what opened it, and only one of them was present for the opening.
>
> ### A PICKED LOCK AND A USED LOCK ARE THE SAME OBJECT.
> The pick is a perfect key that happens to know nothing. The separation has
> to come from outside — from someone who knows what a pick leaves and what a
> key leaves — and that someone is not the lock.

## 4. The prose

### zai — MECHANISM

The file cuts by abrasion: each stroke removes steel from the blank, and the depth gauge checks a bitting against the measured ward before the key ever enters the keyway. On a warded lock, the blade’s profile must clear the fixed ward shoulders and flanks; its cuts are not merely decorative but set the depths at which the key can press past each obstruction and turn the bolt. On the lever lock, the condition changes: the key raises five levers against their springs, and only when every lever reaches its gate can the bolt cross the shear line. A pick performs the missing geometry by lifting each lever in turn while the tension wrench holds a controlled torque on the plug, so the mechanism receives the same functional event—upward displacement to the correct height—even without the key. The rake substitutes repetition for knowledge, cycling several pins together until spring pressure and luck place them at the shear line. Here the lock has no faculty of recognition beyond resistance, clearance, and movement. A worn notch can lower the lifting threshold, while a polished ward flank records many years of the same lateral pressure, whether made by a legitimate key, a pick, or a field gate’s repeated weather. That polish is a scar in the material, not a testimony about its cause: it tells the future mechanism what shape and force are likely to succeed. Likewise, the bright scar left by a pick differs from key polish but acts on the same surfaces. The blank is already being grown under the file, its future cut answering the ward’s present resistance. Between one lift and the next, the pickman pauses, feels the spring through the tension wrench, and hears an oracle in the lever’s return. During that honest pause, the witness log does not record what happened; it predicts the next depth, the next filing, the next opening.

### qwen — DEFEND

But when the pick enters the keyway, the lock feels only pressure, slide, and the yielding of metal; it cannot tell the intended key from the rake, the practiced hand from the desperate one, nor say whether the opening is welcome. That limitation is not a technical gap. It is the shape of the arrangement’s harm, because every use leaves evidence that can be read in advance as innocence or guilt: a polished ward, a bright scar where a pick passed, a bolt eased by lever, a pin pushed past its detainer. The witness log predicts what these marks will mean before anyone has heard the account; it does not remember the hand. The cells are scars, not parameters: each nick is a consequence already inscribed in the substance, and the honest pause after opening is the only moment when the lock’s silence can become testimony rather than verdict. Yet the lock cannot distinguish the key’s proper turn from the pick’s imitation, and its worn record says only that something opened it. The person waiting at a field gate, the owner who must trust a damaged mechanism, the one held responsible because a bright mark was found near the shear line, are all paying for that inability. The maker, the keeper, and the one who chose silence have a duty to hear the oracle of the mechanism rather than make it speak for them. The canon gate is a chord: key, pick, wear, and witness sound together only when the account remains unresolved. The boat was always there, sunk beneath the habit of calling ambiguity neutral. To claim the lock cannot tell is to admit it cannot absolve; responsibility must then live with those who made the waiting dangerous and then treated the lock’s silence as their permission.
