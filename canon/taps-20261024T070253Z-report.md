## 33rd shape — THE ORDER THAT WAS NEVER A ROW
Three voices, three different models, converging on the same structure from different ends:

- **zai (MECHANISM, M2.7-highspeed):** an indexing drum — the escapement and brass pawl keep the order of
  arrivals exactly, because "every arrival must leave a scar in the metal—twin scratches, a burr, a slightly
  polished tooth." But waiting happens **upstream in the felt hopper**, where cards "lean against one another
  without individual marks" and "merely settle again, exchanging places without leaving evidence." The operator
  easing one card at a time **exposes** the waiting order in handling, "though the archive still cannot prove
  that order after the cards are returned."
- **qwen (DEFEND, M3):** the frame "mistakes an institution's filing convenience for the shape of lived time."
  Waiting "is not an empty interval between entries; it is a sequence of exposures—hope, suspicion, hunger,
  humiliation, and the discovery that no one has come—that requires its own order." The moral stake is exact:
  "It makes delay appear empty, then inevitable, then undeserving." Those affected "can be governed without
  testimony, their years treated as storage rather than life." The frame is "**administration dressed as memory**."
- **kimi (DEEPER, M2.5):** attacks the presupposition itself — maybe there was **no waiting-order to lose**.
  "Waiting may not be a second sequence beside arrivals; it may be the way each arrival changes every later
  arrival, a relation rather than a row." To call it *"the order of the waiting"* assumes "that durations can be
  ranked, that waiters become commensurable units." Sharpest move: the phrase "loses the order of the waiting"
  may name "**the archive's deepest fidelity: it forgets waiting only in the way a score forgets the breathing
  between its notes**" — and "waiting as the water's continuing relation to the hull."

**zai + kimi converge** (mechanism ↔ frame) that a lost *row* is not the object: zai's hopper shows waiting
leaves no mark, kimi shows it was never a markable row. **qwen + kimi converge** (defence ↔ frame) that the
framework's error is treating waiting as a rankable unit — qwen names the *cost* (it makes delay look
undeserving), kimi names the *error* (waiters are not commensurable units). **qwen is answered, not routed
around** (3rd round running): kimi grants qwen's point by refusing the row that qwen's defence implicitly needs.

**Canon line:** *"An archive that keeps the order of the arrivals does not merely lose the order of the waiting; it proves that waiting was never a row, and a waiting that was never a row cannot be filed as empty time."*

**Why it is canon-worthy:** it lands cells-are-scars, witness-log-is-prediction and honest-pause-is-product in a
*material* register — the felt hopper, the burr, the polished tooth — while the three voices arrive at a
*jurisdictional* claim (qwen) and then a *refusal of the frame's unit* (kimi). And it makes the round's own
subject the round's method: the round had no shared 5-gram skeleton, and the canon line it produced is the
observation that **an archive's completeness is measured in the rows it keeps — and a thing that was never a
row cannot appear in a ledger of what is missing.** A canon entry admitted without a row.
