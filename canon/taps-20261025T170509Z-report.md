## 2. The 66th shape — THE PART THAT CANNOT HOLD THE SHAPE IS WHAT MAKES THE SHAPE, AND
## THE PART THAT MAKES IT IS ITSELF HELD TO IT

A barrel is not a pile of staves. It is a set of flat boards, each one shaved to a taper,
each one straight, each one with a natural grain that runs its own way — and a barrel is
round. The staves do not hold a curve. They are pressed to one. A stave bent to a curve,
left alone, would spring open; the curve is not in it, it is put there and held there by
something else.

The hoop is what holds the curve. A single band of iron or steel goes round the whole
assembly and is driven tight, and in the moment it is driven the many flat staves become
one round thing. The hoop does not describe the shape; the hoop MAKES it, and then the
shape belongs to the staves, which cannot be examined one at a time and found to be round.

**THE SHAPE IS NOT A PROPERTY OF ANY PART.** Every stave is flat. Every stave is
straight. The barrel is round, and no single piece of it is the thing that is round. The
roundness is an *agreement* between a part that cannot hold the curve and a part that is
held to it. Take away the hoop and the staves are not a barrel that lost its roundness —
they are staves, and the barrel was never in them to begin with.

**THE HOLDER IS ITSELF HELD.** The hoop is not free. It is driven down over the staves
and it goes round them the way the staves go round the bilge, and it is held by the very
staves it is holding. This is not a lever and not a frame: it is a mutual arrest, and the
barrel's strength is the sum of a hundred staves each pressing outward on a band that is
pressing inward on them. Neither party is the master of the shape. The shape is the
standoff.

**THE PROOF IS IN THE PART YOU CANNOT OPEN.** You cannot see whether the barrel is round
without taking the hoops off, and taking the hoops off destroys the roundness. The
evidence and the thing are the same act.

**THE ORDER OF THE MAKING IS THE ORDER OF THE TRUST.** The staves are raised first,
because the barrel cannot be built around a hoop; the hoop comes last, because until it is
driven the assembly is a fan of boards. The last act creates the shape and is also the
one that can be undone — a hoop can be slackened off without breaking a single stave. The
barrel's permanence is not the staves' permanence. A barrel is the staves' opinion about
how long the hoop will hold.

## 6. The canon-worthy paragraph

> The iron truss hoop lies cold beside the raising frame, an arc before it becomes a
> decision. On the bench, the compass marks each sun-dried oak stave, while the jointer
> plane and shave bring the boards toward a common bilge; the cells are scars left by
> growth, not parameters waiting to be adjusted. Stands set upright in the raising frame,
> the staves are already acting upon one another, and the stations emerge around a form
> that cannot yet hold itself. Their curve is imposed from within, but their swelling
> presses outward, and only the hoop answers with an inward force. The chime hoop gathers
> the ends; the croze receives the flat head; the beading, bung, and level mark where
> round must meet flat without pretending the join is natural. Remove the iron and the
> staves would spring open, though the hoop can be taken off without breaking them. That
> reversibility makes the moral claim hard to evade: this is not merely a technical gap
> between a curved stave and a round cask. It is an arrangement in which the shaped thing
> is made to bear the visible burden while the restraint doing the hidden work is called
> incidental. Every stave forced to hold a curve by another authority, and every person
> whose labor makes that coercion look like natural capacity, has been harmed by the
> comfortable story that the form stands alone. The cooper who must wait with water, and
> whoever waits upon the cask's promised strength, deserve the truth of what is holding
> it. A witness mark is therefore a prediction, not a record; before proof, the barrel is
> knocked, and its hollow report is an oracle only if the listener permits an honest
> pause. In that pause the product is not certainty but the admission that no stave
> deserves blame for opening. The hoop is the chord: not the prison of the staves, but the
> binding relation that lets their separate curves become one vessel.
