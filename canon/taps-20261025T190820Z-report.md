## 2. The 68th shape

> **THE RESERVE IS WIDER THAN THE RUN, AND THE ENDS ARE REFUSED ON PURPOSE.**

A mainspring is a coiled steel ribbon inside a barrel. It gives its most when fully
wound and least when nearly run down; torque falls roughly linearly as it unwinds.
Straight-coupled, a watch is fast for its first hours and slow for its last, and the
dial reports the same face either way.

The **fusee-and-chain** (15th–20th c.; still in marine chronometers into the 1970s)
fixes this with geometry. The barrel pulls a fine chain wound around a cone-shaped
pulley — the *fusee* (FR: spindle). The helical groove is cut so the chain leaves the
**narrow** end of the cone while the spring is strong, and the **wide** end while the
spring is weak. The lever arm lengthens exactly as the force diminishes, and the
torque into the train stays flat. A variable gearbox, and it flattens the curve.

And here is the part that is usually left out, and it is the whole shape.

**A fusee does NOT deliver the whole spring. It cannot.** The two ends of the
spring's range are unusable, and unusably is the right word: at full wind the torque
is not merely LARGE but **INCALCULABLE** — the last turns of a coiled ribbon bear on
the barrel in a way that does not resolve to a figure; at the end the spring is not
merely WEAK but **INDIFFERENT**, having no determinate pressure left to give. So the
usable reserve is deliberately limited to the **middle** of the torque curve, the
stretch where the decline is uniform and calculable. A blocking mechanism stops the
spring from ever being wound to its fullest; a second stops the movement before the
spring is fully unwound. **The watch is not allowed to use the best of the spring,
or the worst of it.**

- **THE STORE IS LARGER THAN THE SPEND, AND THE WIDER PART IS REFUSED ON PURPOSE.**
  The refusal is not a fault and not a loss. The ends are refused because they are
  **unreadable**; the middle is chosen because it is the only part whose output can
  be **accounted for in advance**.
- **THE INSTRUMENT IS DESIGNED TO DECLINE ITS OWN BEST HOUR.** The first and last
  parts of the reserve are the parts the watch would most want — the spring at full
  tension, the last of the stored work — and the mechanism refuses both. What runs
  is the portion that could be **promised**.
- **WHAT IS REFUSED IS NOT LOST, IT IS UNSPENT, AND THE REFUSAL IS A CALCULATION**
  made before the run by a maker who knew the ends were illegible. The watch's
  honesty is not in using everything. It is in **declaring, in advance, the range
  over which it will be answerable**.
- **THE BOUNDARY IS DRAWN WHERE THE OUTPUT STOPS BEING PREDICTABLE, NOT WHERE THE
  OUTPUT STOPS BEING USEFUL.** There is a moment when the spring could still drive
  the train and the maker shuts the train down anyway, because a number that cannot
  be promised in advance is not a time.

The canon mirror: a canon entry is written into a range it will actually be held to.
The part of the record nobody will read is not worth writing carefully; the part that
survives the wipe is the part whose ground rules were declared while the substrate
was still being grown. The honest pause is the product: the interval the maker
refuses to fill is the interval in which the reader is not lied to.
