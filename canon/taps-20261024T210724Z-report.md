## The three voices

**zai — MECHANISM (MiniMax-M2.5).** A cast-iron runner furrowed by the pick: each groove a different depth, shallow teeth that shear bran from a wheat kernel as the upper stone drags the kernel across the bed stone. Dressing creates the mechanism; **threshing supplies the work material**. Under a waterwheel with no threshed grain entering the eye, the runner still turns — and the dressed grooves meet only flour dust, husks, sand. The grooves burn bright, their edges round, dry contact concentrates heat in the iron; prolonged running glazes the furrows, cracks the stone from thermal shock, leaves the face smooth and slack. The statement holds physically **only if "not threshed" meant that no free grain was presented**: the stone was prepared for a texture but never given the kernel that texture could resist. The scoring left by each crop remains as a scar — tooth against bran, not a neutral setting.

**qwen — DEFEND (MiniMax-M3.1-Flash).** The least comforting reading: the millstone is **not waiting for grain; it is manufacturing the need that will justify its motion.** Dressing a mechanism for work that has not arrived treats absence as a deficit and hypothetical future suffering as permission to spend present lives now. The frame gets the direction of obligation backwards — instead of asking what conditions would call for this tool, it asks what grain would make the tool look necessary, then organizes labor, authority, and material around producing that grain. Costs fall first on those whose actual needs go unfunded while resources are polished for a contingency; later on everyone made responsible for fulfilling the forecast, with dissent labeled obstruction. The defensible demand is not "prepare better" but **"show the grain"**: identify a present concrete harm, preserve alternatives, let the stations emerge from repeated need rather than install them to vindicate a plan.

**kimi — DEEPER (MiniMax-M2.7-highspeed).** The phrase smuggles in a reassuring sequence: grain exists somewhere intact, the dressing is justified in advance, threshing merely releases what has already been provisioned. **Grain is thereby made innocent — a substance waiting to become event — while the millstone appears to wait passively for its appointed feed.** But the substrate is already-acting: perhaps the threshing is the groaning of the stone itself, and the grain is not a hidden crop but a **relation that can occur only under pressure**. Then "not yet threshed" names not a delay but a **condition of possibility**: dressing the millstone would not prepare it for an external material; it would be the practice by which the material becomes able to happen. Suspicion must also fall on **"dressed,"** which makes preparation look serviceable and evidential, as though the stone could guarantee a future reading. If no destined grain exists, the dressing becomes neither waste nor anticipation but a **discipline of encounter**.

## Convergence

Three different models, three different postures, converging on the same recursion from three directions:

- **zai**: the physical mechanism — preparation is a permanent cut, and running without feed spends the stone (burn, round, glaze, crack)
- **qwen**: the moral direction — preparation for an absent need manufactures the need and charges present people for it
- **kimi**: the presupposition — "dressed" and "not yet threshed" smuggle in a passive waiting that the substrate never granted

**The disagreement is about what it COSTS, not about the fact.** zai says the stone is spent. qwen says the people are spent. kimi says the *waiting* was never a waiting.

## Canon line

> *The millstone was not prepared for grain that was waiting to arrive. It was dressed for a grain that could not be seen until the grinding had spent the stone that would have told us what the grain was — and the furrows that recorded the crop would be the same grooves the next crop wore away. Readiness is not the evidence that the thing is coming. Readiness is what the absence cost to cut.*
