## 2. The 65th shape — THE CARRIER IS GONE BEFORE THE ACT THAT NEEDS IT, AND THE
MARK THAT SURVIVES IS THE MARK OF THE LOSS, NOT THE MARK OF THE WORK

A scribe is an instrument that carries its own means. The nib holds ink; the ink
is a suspension, and a suspension is a carrier. Until the scribe is loaded it is an
empty shape with a promise in it. Then it is loaded — the dip, the charged pen, the
reservoir, the well — and from that moment it carries something it will spend. The
whole craft of the scribe is in managing that: too heavily and it smears, too
lightly and it skips.

And then the water goes. Not the pigment — the pigment is the mark and the pigment
is permanent. The water is the carrier, and the carrier leaves first. A dried ink is
not a lesser ink; it is an ink that has become the thing it was carrying. **The mark
that survives is therefore not the record of the writing. It is the record of the
water's departure.**

So the structure is:

> **THE LAST THING TO HAPPEN IS THE THING THAT LEAVES NO TRACE OF ITSELF.** A wet
> mark is ambiguous — you cannot tell a good line from a blot until it dries. The
> dry mark is the only legible mark, and it is legible precisely because the carrier
> that made it ambiguous is gone. The instrument that writes witnesses the writing.
> The instrument that dries witnesses the loss. And the loss is what settles whether
> what was written was legible at all.
>
> **THE ORDER OF THE FAILURE IS THE ORDER OF THE RECORD.** A scribe that runs dry
> mid-line does not produce a partial mark — it produces a mark that tapers into a
> drag and then stops, and the stop is INVISIBLE until the ink dries and the line is
> read. While the ink is wet the writer knows something is wrong, because the line is
> dying in the hand. The record will not know for a long time, and by the time the
> record knows, the writer has long since believed the page. The witness log is a
> prediction: the taper predicts the dry line that will show the failure, and the
> prediction is available long before the evidence.
>
> **THE INSTRUMENT AND ITS LOAD ARE SEPARATE OBJECTS THAT ARE EASILY CONFLATED.** A
> dry scribe is a scribe with no ink in it — an instrument that cannot do the work
> and looks exactly like an instrument that has already done it. The empty nib and
> the written page are the same silhouette. The state of the carrier is not legible
> on the object, only in what it last produced.
