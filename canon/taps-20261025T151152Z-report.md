## 2. The 64th shape — THE PASS THAT LEAVES NO WITNESS OF ITSELF DECIDES THE RESULT

A blade comes up over a sequence of passes, and each pass removes metal and each
pass leaves a witness of itself: the file leaves its tooth marks, the grindstone
its arc across the bevel, the hone a straight line down the flat. A sharpener
reads the edge the way a mason reads a dressed face — by the marks of the tools
that have been over it. Those marks are the record of the work, and they are
legible.

Then the strop. A strop is a strip of leather charged with rouge; the edge is
drawn along it and it is **NOT CUT**. The strop removes no metal and leaves no
mark of its own — no line, no arc, no tooth track. So the final state of the edge,
whether it will hold a keen edge or fold into a wire burr at the first cut, is
settled by the ONE operation in the sequence that **cannot be seen in the record**.
Every visible mark on the edge belongs to a pass that did not decide the outcome.

**Three claims, and the inversion at the centre of them:**

1. **THE LAST PASS IS NOT THE ONE THAT SHOWS.** The evidence is legible and
   abundant, and it is evidence about the *earlier* passes. The deciding pass is
   the only one with no witness, because it is the only one that does not remove
   material. A record made of tool marks cannot record the step that changed
   nothing — and the step that changed nothing is the step that decided.
2. **THE PASS THAT CHANGES NOTHING IS NOT THEREFORE THE PASS THAT MATTERS
   NOTHINGS.** "It left no mark" is normally a way of saying it did no work.
   Here the strop is the whole work: it draws the wire edge down into a single
   line instead of a row of microscopic teeth, and a row of microscopic teeth is
   a burr that will fold on the first cut. **The absence of a mark is the evidence
   of the presence of the effect**, and the record cannot carry that inversion.
3. **THE RECORD AND THE OBJECT DISAGREE ABOUT WHAT IS EVIDENCE.** The edge is
   read as the sum of what was done to it. The edge is actually the state left by
   the *last* thing done to it — and the last thing left no mark, so the reading
   understates the last act and overstates the earlier ones.
