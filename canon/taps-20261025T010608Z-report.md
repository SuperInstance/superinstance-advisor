A collimated monochromatic beam illuminates the air gap between the two faces, and light reflected from those faces interferes as a pattern of dark and bright fringes. Since the phase difference depends on twice the local air-gap thickness, a plane test surface combined with uniform wedge and tilt produces straight, evenly spaced fringes; a local high or low spot bends or displaces them. Counting the fringes crossed while the wedge moves gives the gap change in multiples of the wavelength, while tilting the plate changes the wedge angle, fringe spacing, and shear of the wavefront phase gradient, so a tilt must not be mistaken for a surface error. The witness log is prediction: the expected fringe count, spacing, and coincidence point are recorded beforehand so the observed pattern can test them. The honest pause is the product of the null, because a flat reading says only that no disagreement from the reference was resolved.
