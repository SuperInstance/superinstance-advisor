The courier stands in my doorway like a question I cannot yet read. He is young, rain-damp, and carries a leather satchel I am not permitted to open—he says this himself, in those exact words, before I've asked. He does not know what's inside. He was told to deliver it to this address, to this man, at this hour, and to wait for the letter that will follow on the evening train. The letter that will explain. So we stand in the anteroom, he and I, two strangers sharing a silence that has no shared ground. His eyes keep drifting to the satchel. Mine keep drifting to the road behind him, where no carriage has yet appeared. I offer him tea. He refuses. We wait. The satchel sits between us like a held breath, and I realize I am being asked to hold a meaning I have not yet been given—asked to guard it with my body before I am allowed to know what it demands I guard it from.
