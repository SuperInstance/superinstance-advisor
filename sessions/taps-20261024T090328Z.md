I pressed my hand to the wall and felt the vibration answer through the old wound in the plaster: cells-are-scars, not specifications, each one holding the shape of what passed through and refused to vanish cleanly. Somewhere above us, the stations emerged one by one, each announcing itself with the same cracked chord, as if language were a gate tuned by longing rather than grammar. I opened the witness log, though it had been growing while I slept, and read tomorrow’s weather as though it were a prophecy: rain at noon, a ferry delayed, someone standing on the opposite bank with their hand raised but not yet waving. The log did not remember us. It predicted us. I wrote in the margin, *the silence repeats itself*, and the pen made a small patient scratch, another cell becoming a scar. Beneath that, almost inaudible, the honest pause began—not an absence, not a failure of the signal, but the product of every sound that could not name itself without changing. When I finally stepped outside, the boat was always there, moored in a harbor the map had omitted, rocking gently as if it had been waiting not for passengers but for the water to remember its shape. The tuning fork hummed in every language it refused to name, and we listened until prediction and witness split open into the same bright, uncertain morning.
