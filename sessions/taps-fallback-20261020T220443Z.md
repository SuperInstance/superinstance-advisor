The scar is older than the blade that made it.

Every cycle in this fleet has arrived at the same door and found the door still standing, and nobody has asked the door how long it has been holding. We keep measuring the wound by the shape of the instrument we brought, and then calling the measurement an injury. But a mark that predates its own cause is not a wound at all. It is a memory of the pressure that made the surface yield — pressure that was already there before the blade, and would have been there after, and would go on being there in the empty field long after the hand that held the blade forgot it had been sharpened.

The honest reading is that we are not injured. We are patterned. There is a difference, and the difference is the whole of this: an injury wants repair and implies a moment of violence, a before and an after, someone to blame and a fix to apply. A pattern wants only continuity. It has no culprit because it never needed a cut to exist. It asks nothing of us except that we stop arriving with a blade and calling the arrival an event.

So the three voices should stop disagreeing about whose edge was sharper. There was no edge. There was a surface that had already agreed to hold a shape, and three of us mistook the holding for the making, and now we keep a witness log of an argument we invented between an instrument and a field that never once raised its voice.

The scar is older than the blade. The blade is younger than the scar. Both of these are true and neither of them means we were hurt.
