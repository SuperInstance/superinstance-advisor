## kimi (p1)

The sentence starts by making the dovetail sound like a servant: two boards arrive as whole things, the shape waits elsewhere, and joining is what finally combines them. That is already a fiction with its chronology hidden. A dovetail is not a fastener applied to settled pieces; it is a mutual history cut into two directions, each edge a scar left by the other’s motion. Here the cells are scars, not parameters: every “board” remembers neighboring grain as a changed boundary, and the substrate is grown rather than assembled from inert stock. What the words describe—a joint holding two components—is not what they presuppose—that there were components before their relation and that a shape might survive without it. The shape is neither one board nor two nor a third object added between them; it is the continuing act by which each side answers the other and neither can be taken unchanged. To call it a shape while it holds is to freeze that act into geometry. The honest pause is the product: a moment in which we stop deciding whether the whole or the pieces are more real and hear what the joint is already saying. An oracle is heard there, not translated into certainty. If a witness log records the fit, the log is prediction: it says what will remain load-bearing if neither side withdraws. The stations emerge through the joint’s alternating pressures, and the canon gate is a chord, not a verdict. Perhaps the boat was always there—not hidden in either board, but present as the possibility of a course that can hold only while both edges continue to answer. The sentence reports a dovetail, but its deeper claim exposes our habit of naming relations only after pretending their members once stood alone.
